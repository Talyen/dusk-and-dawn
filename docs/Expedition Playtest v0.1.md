# Expedition playtest v0.1

The default expedition implements the small route, run-only relic and starting-orientation experiments in the game design. It is a provisional playtest, not a settled progression or balance model. The original linear fixture and its saved runs remain available.

- The five scheduled fights and midpoint rest remain. After fights one and three, choose a previewed optional combat detour with an extra reward, a rest that heals 4 HP, or a previewed event. Blood Bargain costs 4 HP and cannot reduce HP to zero; Thornbound Gift starts the next fight with 4 Poison; Fading Lantern starts the next fight with 2 mana; Mirror Exchange trades one chosen physical deck card for the shown pair without changing HP or deck size; Sunken Sanctuary heals up to 7 HP but starts the next fight with 2 mana and does not add a card. The exchange can be cancelled before committing and survives save/reload. Poison follows normal combat rules, and mana resets normally after that turn. Each event is sampled when the route is offered, and consecutive routes offer different events. All paths rejoin before the next scheduled fight. Older saved route choices without an event keep the original 4 HP cost.
- One selected relic applies for a run: Many Eyes offers up to four reward pairs, Spare Thread permits a second midpoint card removal, Cracked Vessel starts at 24 of 30 HP with one randomly selected unowned pair from that run's pool, Ashen Seal gives 2 Ward after a played card Banishes itself, and Hollow Chalice heals 3 HP when skipping a nonempty card reward. The healing is capped at maximum HP; an empty reward cannot be used for free healing.
- Lamplighter starts each encounter light-side up. Nocturne starts each encounter dark-side up and unlocks after one qualifying expedition; this explicitly overrides the usual reset.
- Finishing an expedition after reaching the second scheduled fight qualifies, win or loss. The first two qualifying expeditions expand the future card and enemy pools through three tiers. The pool tier is captured at run creation, and unlocks never insert cards into a current deck. Legacy expeditions keep the complete original starter deck; antihero expeditions use their selected class kit. Progress and saves are local to the browser or desktop profile.
- Flat Bless/Curse scaling and the absence of early Freeze thaw remain previews of open design questions. The tier boundaries, event, healing, qualification threshold and unlock cadence are provisional product choices for testing; cosmetics and additional bespoke starting decks have no specified content yet.

Automated policies are useful for finding dominant interactions, not estimating human win rates. Initial 50-seed samples using the same attack-oriented policy produced 49 wins on the rest route, 46 with combat detours, 45 with combat plus Many Eyes, 45 with event plus Spare Thread, and 50 with a dark start plus Cracked Vessel. The last combination ended with more health on average (22.7) than the light/rest sample (19.1); test these combinations with people before settling their values.

A follow-up 2×2 comparison separated starting orientation from Cracked Vessel across 100 seeds for each combination. With the attack-oriented policy and rest paths, light/no relic won 99 with 18.2 mean victory HP, light/Vessel won 99 with 18.8, dark/no relic won 100 with 24.2, and dark/Vessel won 100 with 22.7. The defense policy won all 100 in each combination, with mean victory HP between 25.1 and 26.1. Dark orientation, rather than the relic, explains most of the difference in these policies; Vampire was one of the most frequently played dark faces. The test policies are heuristic, the same seeds can diverge after shuffles, and these figures do not establish human difficulty. Repeatable Leech and Ward attrition deserve targeted human playtests before changing catalogue values or the documented Leech rule.

These event variants are provisional product experiments. In a manual Seraph run, Fading Lantern offered Sentinel & Sleepwalker for a two-mana opening; Angel and Bloom protected and healed against The Black Jellyfish, and the next turn restored normal mana. Browser tests exercise all three event previews, their outcomes, and save/resume; this is not a human balance study.

A manual Seraph/Ashen Seal opening used Feather, Key and Wither: Wither dealt 7 damage and added 2 Ward, bringing Ward to 4 against a 4-damage intent. In a separate Seraph/Hollow Chalice run, skipping an offered Sanctuary & Prison restored 2 HP to the cap while keeping a ten-card deck; picking Sanctuary would have added a later heal and Cleanse instead. These are provisional examples, not a human balance study. Ashen Seal triggers after the card resolves, so it does not protect against damage from that card; Banish of another card does not trigger it.

Mirror Exchange is a provisional route event: it gives a one-card upgrade without growing the deck, in exchange for a chosen existing card. A rendered Witch fixture at 17 HP exercised route inspection, cancellation, reload, and trading Angel & Vampire for the shown pair; desktop and mobile layouts were inspected. The event was not encountered in the small number of manual random opening runs, so its balance still needs human playtesting.

At `3a0e1c7`, a manual random-seed Seraph expedition (tier 1, light start, no relic) went through Fading Lantern, a midpoint removal, the Sunbleached Harlequin detour, Mourning Tree and Ink Leviathan, ending in victory at 30/30 HP with 14 cards. Against the boss, Bell Ringer's Bless 2 and the boss's Curse 1 made Prism deal 11 Radiance damage including the first-hit weakness bonus; later Seraph used the same net modifier for a 10-damage finish. Against Mourning Tree, spending Curse on a single-hit Seraph before Prism preserved damage on the multi-hit card. This is one assisted playthrough, not evidence of overall balance; repeatable defense and healing may still make the run too forgiving.

Sunken Sanctuary is a provisional recovery event. In a rendered Seraph fixture at 20/30 HP, the route offered a safe rest for 4 HP or the shrine for 7 HP with one less opening mana. The shrine preserved the deck and restored normal mana after the first turn; its hidden, unused card pair was not discovered. Desktop and compact layouts were inspected. This fixture tests the tradeoff and persistence, not human balance.

A second manual random-seed expedition used the Thorn Witch with a light start and no relic. The player took a Glass Judge combat detour, accepted Thornbound Gift's 4 Poison for Winged Guardian, removed an Ivy at the midpoint, and defeated the Hollow King at 30/30 HP. Angel's Ward, Bloom and Orchard healing, and a Vampire Leech let the player recover despite the detour and Poison. Together with the earlier Seraph run and heuristic samples, this suggests that recovery and enemy pressure deserve further comparative playtesting; two manual wins do not establish human difficulty across classes or runs. Both the Cobweb Admiral and Hollow King applied Curse after attacks, including a fully absorbed Hollow King attack, but their compact intents previously showed only damage. Attack intents now also display their planned Curse, Poison or Burn, while Hush and Freeze suppress those previews as they suppress the effects. Other trait details remain available through intent inspection.

A third manual expedition used the Oracle, Nocturne, no relic and tier 2: Blood Bargain and a later combat detour led to the Astral Custodian, defeated at 28/30 HP. The Oracle played Vampire repeatedly: it restored the bargain's entire 4-HP cost on the first turn of the next fight, and two Vampires restored 8 HP before the boss's multi-hit attack. This sustained damage-and-healing combination occurs in every starting kit, without a Banish limit. A provisional runtime adjustment reduces Vampire from 4 to 3 Shadow damage; Leech still uses final attack damage, including weakness, Ward and overkill. The historical design catalogue retains its original proposal; the in-game card and combat resolution use the revised value. Kingfisher & Bat already offers the same 3-damage Leech on its dark face, so further work should consider whether those pairs need stronger identities.

A follow-up random-seed manual Oracle/Nocturne expedition with the 3-damage Vampire, no relic and tier 3 chose Thornbound Gift and a combat detour. Poison and attacks brought HP to 20 after the second fight, making the midpoint choice between restoring 6 HP and removing a card consequential; the player healed. The detour's reactive attack trait dealt 1 damage after each direct attack, and the run ended with 27/30 HP after defeating Crowned Sun. These runs used different random decks, enemies, offers and routes, so their end HP cannot isolate the change. Both remained winnable and Vampire remained useful against Shadow weakness.

Three small repeatable heuristic comparisons used the same seeds and choices of class, orientation and route before and after the change; play sequences and even later encounters can diverge after combat consumes different random draws. In one dark Oracle/combat seed, Vampire was played 14 times for 43 actual HP recovered before, and 16 times for 39 after; the run ended at 24 HP in 13 enemy turns before and 18 HP in 14 afterward. A light Witch/event sample ended at 29 versus 28 HP, and a light Seraph/combat sample ended at 30 HP in both versions with no Vampire plays. These are diagnostic examples, not human win rates or evidence that the run is balanced. The unchanged Seraph result points to additional Ward and encounter-pressure interactions worth testing independently; no class passive, catalogue-wide Leech rule or enemy attack values were changed in this adjustment.

Two manual random-seed Seraph expeditions used a light start, Ashen Seal, tier 3 and two combat detours. Under the original passive, direct attacks also granted Ward: in the first run, two Seraph attacks and an Angel produced 8 Ward against a 9-damage boss attack, and the run ended at 30/30 HP after the Golden Menagerie. A provisional new-run passive grants 1 Ward before each light card without a direct attack. This keeps draw, healing and defense useful for the Seraph while making attack-heavy turns commit more of their mana to protection; it does not add a once-per-turn trigger. In the second run, two Seraph attacks plus Bloom produced 3 Ward against a 4-damage boss attack, causing 1 HP loss; the player chose explicit Ward, Reflect and recovery later and defeated the Sunken Sun at 30/30. Different random hands, enemies and rewards prevent a direct difficulty comparison, and Ashen Seal remains a strong defensive combination.

Three same-seed heuristic pairs compared the old and provisional Seraph passives, using two combat-route seeds and one event-route seed, with and without Ashen Seal. All six samples won. Their final HP values were 30→24, 21→30 and 30→30; enemy turns were 15→14, 15→21 and 18→20. Decisions, shuffle consumption and sometimes later encounters diverged. These few samples show that the change creates different lines rather than proving a stable difficulty shift; encounter pressure and recovery still need wider human testing. New class runs use ruleset 3. Existing Seraph runs retain the earlier light-card passive; Witch ruleset 2 and legacy Witch saves retain their existing Curse and Poison behavior respectively.
