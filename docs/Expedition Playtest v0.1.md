# Expedition playtest v0.1

The default expedition implements the small route, run-only relic and starting-orientation experiments in the game design. It is a provisional playtest, not a settled progression or balance model. The original linear fixture and its saved runs remain available.

- The five scheduled fights and midpoint rest remain. After fights one and three, choose a previewed optional combat detour with an extra reward, a rest that heals up to 4 HP, or a previewed event. Blood Bargain costs 4 HP and cannot reduce HP to zero; Thornbound Gift starts the next fight with 4 Poison; Fading Lantern starts the next fight with 2 mana; Mirror Exchange trades one chosen physical deck card for the shown pair without changing HP or deck size; Sunken Sanctuary heals up to 7 HP but starts the next fight with 2 mana and does not add a card. The exchange can be cancelled before committing and survives save/reload. Poison follows normal combat rules, and mana resets normally after that turn. Each event is sampled when the route is offered, and consecutive routes offer different events. A completed first detour is excluded from the later detour offer; an enemy offered but not fought remains eligible. All paths rejoin before the next scheduled fight. Older saved route choices without an event keep the original 4 HP cost.
- One selected relic applies for a run: Many Eyes offers up to four reward pairs, Spare Thread permits a second midpoint card removal, Cracked Vessel starts at 24 maximum HP with one randomly selected unowned pair from that run's pool, Ashen Seal gives 2 Ward after a played card Banishes itself, and Hollow Chalice heals 3 HP when skipping a nonempty card reward. The healing is capped at maximum HP; an empty reward cannot be used for free healing.
- Lamplighter starts each encounter light-side up. Nocturne starts each encounter dark-side up and unlocks after one qualifying expedition; this explicitly overrides the usual reset.
- Tier 1 expeditions and classic runs open against Radiant Moth. Tier 2 adds the Ink Jackrabbit and Blue Jackal as possible opening enemies; tier 3 also adds the Brass Cicada. These modestly stronger alternatives are provisional and are never duplicated among the remaining scheduled fights. Existing saved runs keep their original encounters.
- Finishing an expedition after reaching the second scheduled fight qualifies, win or loss. The first two qualifying expeditions expand the future card and enemy pools through three tiers. The pool tier is captured at run creation, and unlocks never insert cards into a current deck. Legacy expeditions keep the complete original starter deck; antihero expeditions use their selected class kit. Progress and saves are local to the browser or desktop profile.
- Flat Bless/Curse scaling and the absence of early Freeze thaw remain previews of open design questions. The tier boundaries, event, healing, qualification threshold and unlock cadence are provisional product choices for testing; cosmetics and additional bespoke starting decks have no specified content yet.

Automated policies are useful for finding dominant interactions, not estimating human win rates. Initial 50-seed samples using the same attack-oriented policy produced 49 wins on the rest route, 46 with combat detours, 45 with combat plus Many Eyes, 45 with event plus Spare Thread, and 50 with a dark start plus Cracked Vessel. The last combination ended with more health on average (22.7) than the light/rest sample (19.1); test these combinations with people before settling their values.

A follow-up 2×2 comparison separated starting orientation from Cracked Vessel across 100 seeds for each combination. With the attack-oriented policy and rest paths, light/no relic won 99 with 18.2 mean victory HP, light/Vessel won 99 with 18.8, dark/no relic won 100 with 24.2, and dark/Vessel won 100 with 22.7. The defense policy won all 100 in each combination, with mean victory HP between 25.1 and 26.1. Dark orientation, rather than the relic, explains most of the difference in these policies; Vampire was one of the most frequently played dark faces. The test policies are heuristic, the same seeds can diverge after shuffles, and these figures do not establish human difficulty. Repeatable Leech and Ward attrition deserve targeted human playtests before changing catalogue values or the documented Leech rule.

These event variants are provisional product experiments. In a manual Seraph run, Fading Lantern offered Sentinel & Sleepwalker for a two-mana opening; Angel and Bloom protected and healed against The Black Jellyfish, and the next turn restored normal mana. Browser tests exercise all three event previews, their outcomes, and save/resume; this is not a human balance study.

A manual Seraph/Ashen Seal opening used Feather, Key and Wither: Wither dealt 7 damage and added 2 Ward, bringing Ward to 4 against a 4-damage intent. In a separate Seraph/Hollow Chalice run, skipping an offered Sanctuary & Prison restored 2 HP to the cap while keeping a ten-card deck; picking Sanctuary would have added a later heal and Cleanse instead. These are provisional examples, not a human balance study. Ashen Seal triggers after the card resolves, so it does not protect against damage from that card; Banish of another card does not trigger it.

Mirror Exchange is a provisional route event: it gives a one-card upgrade without growing the deck, in exchange for a chosen existing card. A rendered Witch fixture at 17 HP exercised route inspection, cancellation, reload, and trading Angel & Vampire for the shown pair; desktop and mobile layouts were inspected. The event was not encountered in the small number of manual random opening runs, so its balance still needs human playtesting.

At `3a0e1c7`, a manual random-seed Seraph expedition (tier 1, light start, no relic) went through Fading Lantern, a midpoint removal, the Sunbleached Harlequin detour, Mourning Tree and Ink Leviathan, ending in victory at 30/30 HP with 14 cards. Against the boss, Bell Ringer's Bless 2 and the boss's Curse 1 made Prism deal 11 Radiance damage including the first-hit weakness bonus; later Seraph used the same net modifier for a 10-damage finish. Against Mourning Tree, spending Curse on a single-hit Seraph before Prism preserved damage on the multi-hit card. This is one assisted playthrough, not evidence of overall balance; repeatable defense and healing may still make the run too forgiving.

Sunken Sanctuary is a provisional recovery event. In a rendered Seraph fixture at 20/30 HP, the route offered a safe rest for 4 HP or the shrine for 7 HP with one less opening mana. The shrine preserved the deck and restored normal mana after the first turn; its hidden, unused card pair was not discovered. Desktop and compact layouts were inspected. This fixture tests the tradeoff and persistence, not human balance.

A second manual random-seed expedition used the Thorn Witch with a light start and no relic. The player took a Glass Judge combat detour, accepted Thornbound Gift's 4 Poison for Winged Guardian, removed an Ivy at the midpoint, and defeated the Hollow King at 30/30 HP. Angel's Ward, Bloom and Orchard healing, and a Vampire Leech let the player recover despite the detour and Poison. Together with the earlier Seraph run and heuristic samples, this suggests that recovery and enemy pressure deserve further comparative playtesting; two manual wins do not establish human difficulty across classes or runs. Both the Cobweb Admiral and Hollow King applied Curse after attacks, including a fully absorbed Hollow King attack, but their compact intents previously showed only damage. Attack intents now also display their planned Curse, Poison or Burn, while Hush and Freeze suppress those previews as they suppress the effects. Other trait details remain available through intent inspection.

A third manual expedition used the Oracle, Nocturne, no relic and tier 2: Blood Bargain and a later combat detour led to the Astral Custodian, defeated at 28/30 HP. The Oracle played Vampire repeatedly: it restored the bargain's entire 4-HP cost on the first turn of the next fight, and two Vampires restored 8 HP before the boss's multi-hit attack. This sustained damage-and-healing combination occurs in every starting kit, without a Banish limit. A provisional runtime adjustment reduces Vampire from 4 to 3 Shadow damage; Leech still uses final attack damage, including weakness, Ward and overkill. The historical design catalogue retains its original proposal; the in-game card and combat resolution use the revised value. Kingfisher & Bat already offers the same 3-damage Leech on its dark face, so further work should consider whether those pairs need stronger identities.

A follow-up random-seed manual Oracle/Nocturne expedition with the 3-damage Vampire, no relic and tier 3 chose Thornbound Gift and a combat detour. Poison and attacks brought HP to 20 after the second fight, making the midpoint choice between restoring 6 HP and removing a card consequential; the player healed. The detour's reactive attack trait dealt 1 damage after each direct attack, and the run ended with 27/30 HP after defeating Crowned Sun. These runs used different random decks, enemies, offers and routes, so their end HP cannot isolate the change. Both remained winnable and Vampire remained useful against Shadow weakness.

Three small repeatable heuristic comparisons used the same seeds and choices of class, orientation and route before and after the change; play sequences and even later encounters can diverge after combat consumes different random draws. In one dark Oracle/combat seed, Vampire was played 14 times for 43 actual HP recovered before, and 16 times for 39 after; the run ended at 24 HP in 13 enemy turns before and 18 HP in 14 afterward. A light Witch/event sample ended at 29 versus 28 HP, and a light Seraph/combat sample ended at 30 HP in both versions with no Vampire plays. These are diagnostic examples, not human win rates or evidence that the run is balanced. The unchanged Seraph result points to additional Ward and encounter-pressure interactions worth testing independently; no class passive, catalogue-wide Leech rule or enemy attack values were changed in this adjustment.

Two manual random-seed Seraph expeditions used a light start, Ashen Seal, tier 3 and two combat detours. Different random hands, enemies and rewards prevent a direct difficulty comparison, and Ashen Seal remains a strong defensive combination.

New class runs use ruleset 3. Existing Seraph runs retain the earlier light-card passive; Witch ruleset 2 and legacy Witch saves retain their existing Curse and Poison behavior respectively.

A natural tier 3 dark Witch run under the new rule took both combat detours, declined a 4-HP rest at 25/30, and defeated the Sunken Sun at 29/30. A full run through reward, route, midpoint removal, combat and victory exposed no blocking UI issue in that playthrough.

The flat/percentage choice is still open. A comparison of the existing 25%-per-stack model (combined Bless minus Curse, 0×–2× clamp, nearest integer with half upward) against live flat scaling illustrates the tradeoff: the Golden Menagerie's 3 + 2 + 2 + 2 attack totals 5/1/0 under flat Curse 1/2/3, versus 8/5/4 under the percentage candidate. A 2-damage hit under Curse 1 stays at 2 with that rounding instead of falling to 1, while Bless 1 makes a 6-damage hit 8 rather than 7. The percentage candidate reduces the multi-hit shutdown but creates small-hit dead zones and larger-hit swings; both still reach zero with enough Curse. Recommend a separate, explicitly labeled comparison playtest across single- and multi-hit enemies, Leech, Reflect and several builds before selecting or retuning either model. No live scaling rule was changed in this iteration.

Reproduce the current sample with `node scripts/sample-rewards.mjs 300`.

## Witch pressure and shared recovery comparison

At `4b5fcca`, a fresh natural tier 3 Nocturne Oracle run with no relic defeated the Cracked Moon at 26/30 HP. The route included both Smiling Kiln and Coin-Eyed Stag combat detours; the midpoint healed 6 HP instead of removing a card. The Kiln dealt 6 damage followed by Burn pressure, bringing the Oracle to 19 HP before a Vampire finish restored 3. Against the Linen Lioness, Vampire healed 3 even though its entire hit was absorbed by Ward. The Oracle acquired Rattle and spent a mana on Curse 2 to cut the Salt Crown's 4+2+2 attack to 2. In the boss fight, Scry revealed Vampire on top of the library, so Locust was not played because its cost would have Banished that card; Rattle, Poison and Wither were used instead. These were real choices in one run, not a matched outcome comparison with the earlier Witch run.

The controlled tests in `tests/lib/witch-pressure-comparison.test.ts` give each current ruleset-4 class the same three dark attack cards from 20 HP: Vampire and two Thorns. This deliberately equalizes the hand rather than modeling each class's starting deck. Vampire healed all three classes to 25 against Shadow-weak Brass Cicada or Golden Menagerie, and to 23 against the non-weak Threaded Monarch. The Witch accumulated 3 Curse; Seraph and Oracle accumulated none. After the Cicada's 3+1+1+1 action, Witch remained at 25 while the other classes fell to 19. After the Menagerie's 3+2+2+2 action, Witch remained at 25 while the others fell to 16. Against the Monarch's single 6-damage hit, the corresponding results were 20 and 17. Replacing Vampire with non-Leech Iron Boot left everyone at 20 before the enemy action and produced 20 versus 14, 20 versus 11, and 17 versus 14 respectively. These are selected hands, not class win rates; all-dark attacks do not trigger the Seraph's light-support passive and the Oracle's Scry was not used to change the hand.

Separate matched cases confirm all three classes heal 5 from a Shadow-weak Vampire strike even when five Ward blocks it or the target has only 1 HP; a non-weak strike heals 3 even through five Ward. That is the existing explicitly documented Leech rule, not a defect found here. The evidence points to both shared, repeatable recovery and the Witch's offense-generated Curse: they combine particularly strongly against small multi-hit attacks under the provisional flat model, while single hits remain more threatening. Non-attack enemy turns and healing at rest can also create recovery opportunities. The natural Oracle run shows meaningful pressure and explicit defensive spending without the Witch passive, but its different enemies, draws, rewards and route prevent attributing its final HP to class alone.

No live balance change is justified from these cases alone. Reducing Vampire again would affect all classes and its existing damage identity; restricting the Witch to a narrower subset of attacks would still permit repeated eligible attacks and depends on the unresolved Bless/Curse scaling decision. Keep those decisions open, and use matched full-run human playtests with the same encounters and starting orientations before tuning. The controlled cases protect the current baseline; they are not recommendations to preserve flat scaling.

## Browser loading and run responsiveness

At `3ac96f9` and with the local loading change, a cold Chromium opening at 1280 × 720 used a simulated 1.5 Mbps download and 80 ms latency against the local production preview. In one seeded comparison, the final visible opening card finished about 5.45 seconds after combat began before the change and about 4.85 seconds after it. Class-selection portraits were still downloading during the transition, so the result includes contention from a rapidly advanced setup screen; the local preview uses a different network path from production. Request priority hints alone produced only a small change in this setup. These are single measurements, not a benchmark or a claim about Vercel performance.

The battle now requests its visible hand and enemy at high browser priority and waits for those current images to finish or fail before preloading both faces of the deck at low priority. A played or flipped card can replace an image that is still loading; the watcher follows the current board rather than waiting for the removed image. Background faces start later on slow connections, but a face revealed early is requested by its visible image immediately. Art files and resolution are unchanged.

A separate local, unthrottled, scripted Chromium expedition completed six fights and two route choices with 90 decisions. Over the recorded run, the animation-frame interval was 16.7 ms median and 16.8 ms at the 95th percentile; no sampled interval exceeded 50 ms, no browser long task or page error was reported, and 5 of 326 sampled battle frames included an image that had not loaded. Playwright click calls had a 28 ms median and 246 ms 95th percentile; these include automation actionability checks and are not direct input-to-paint measurements. This is one automated run on one machine, not a substitute for human play or slower-device profiling. Browser regression cases additionally hold a visible image, verify background preloads wait, and flip the hand before the delayed image completes.

A subsequent interactive local Seraph expedition flipped the opening hand, defeated the Radiant Moth, inspected the three illustrated reward pairs, reloaded and resumed the same rewards, selected Lark & Owl, took the healing route, and reloaded into the Coin-Eyed Stag fight. At local network speed the observed hand, rewards and resumed battle showed the correct art. This was a partial run, not a full-run performance or balance test.

A separate controlled Chromium test delayed the Fallen Seraph image while Key flipped Seraph. Chromium retained the old Seraph artwork on the reused image element, although the card had become Fallen Seraph. Card and enemy images now get fresh elements when their face or enemy changes; while an image is pending, a neutral patterned back appears in place of misleading old art. The same background is used behind loading reward and enemy images. A regression verifies that the old face element is detached during the delay and the correct new image appears when released. On a slow connection the neutral back can remain visible until the artwork arrives; card play and inspection remain available.

A follow-up interactive Seraph fight against the Coin-Eyed Stag used Feather and Angel to block its opening 6 damage, Benediction and Seraph for 10 damage, then Key to Flip the hand. The Stag's next attack dealt 2 HP through Ward and applied Curse 1; the following Seraph hit dealt 6 and spent that Curse. Lark then drew from the reshuffled discard, revealing a previously flipped Wither that finished the fight at 28/30 HP. The inspection now reports when Reflect has been spent; it also calls out an active reflection when Freeze skips the enemy action or lethal Burn would end the fight at end turn. A browser regression checks the active and spent states, reloads after the first reflected hit, and verifies Seraph damages the Swan normally. Further checks cover Freeze, lethal Burn, and the existing Hush behavior. These are selected cases, not a complete enemy-interaction audit; no combat rule or Bless/Curse scaling changed.
