3 November 2026 · PROPOSAL, NOT PLAYTESTED
110 / 110 existing enemy artworks inspected; no resistances.
One typed base attack per enemy. Apply the named trait and displayed rhythm.
Weakness: +2 once per played direct-attack card, before Ward; no Burn bonus.
Current Hush cancels displayed non-attack intents only. Weaken reduces the first hit of the next attack.
Light and dark are moods, not moral alignments. All numbers are provisional.

TIMING AND ASSUMPTIONS

• This is a local design proposal for all 110 existing enemy artworks, not runtime content or playtested balance. No main design or card catalogue was edited.
• Each enemy has exactly one named trait, one opposing-theme weakness and one named base attack. Rhythm is the schedule of that trait and attack. Some traits have two phases within one short rhythm. Related materials can share familiar operations; unique means a distinct named rule and encounter combination, not 110 new keywords.
• Attack numbers are provisional damage per hit. An attack has the enemy's single printed type throughout all its hits; added damage and additional hits keep that type. No damage resistance, immunity, retaliation damage or player Burn is introduced.
• A matching direct card attack gets the current +2 weakness bonus once per played card, before Ward. Burn is always Flame and never receives the weakness bonus. Utility themes do not secretly deal damage.
• Latest rule contract: Weaken adds together, persists to the next attack and reduces its first hit only, before Ward. Hush cancels only the currently displayed non-attack intent and advances the pattern; every Hush card Banishes itself. It does not silence passive traits, attacks or attack riders. Any earlier trait-silence sketch is superseded here.
• Design decision to revisit: 90 enemies have no non-attack intent in this proposal, so current Hush is not playable against them. The roster does not assume the broader trait-silence behavior discussed earlier. That would require a separate rules change and new previews.
• Keep follows the updated rule: marked cards survive end-turn discard, lose their markers, and remain in hand; draw up to five new cards next turn within the eight-card cap. A card leaving hand loses its marker. Enemy effects moving a kept card do not Banish it unless Banish is explicitly stated.
• For these proposed triggers, snapshot cards kept this turn immediately after end-turn discard, before their markers clear. A reference to currently kept/carried cards means this snapshot intersected with cards still in hand. A reference to cards carried from the previous turn instead means physical cards already in hand before this turn's normal draw. Show both counts when relevant.
• All player-turn counters record successfully resolved card effects only, including extra plays from mana gain. Normal turn draw and automatic end-turn discard are not draw/discard card effects. Record physical IDs and pair IDs, current face on play, direct damage types, HP payments, HP healing, Ward gained, and the stated history from the immediately previous turn. No comparison is made on turn 1 unless specified.
• Exact order: inspect dynamic intent during the player turn; snapshot/discard the hand; tick and reduce Burn; if the enemy survives, expire its old Ward at the start of its action even if Hushed; resolve either the displayed non-attack action or pre-attack trait, base attack and post-attack trait. Stop immediately if player HP reaches zero. Enemy healing is capped at its configured maximum HP.
• Once an action starts, evaluate each condition at its stated point. A base-hit trigger uses the leading hit after Weaken and Ward, before any extra hit; it cannot trigger itself recursively. Fully absorbed by Ward requires positive damage to have been absorbed and zero HP damage; Weaken reducing a hit to zero alone is not Ward absorption.
• If a pattern advances by turns or attacks, display its step explicitly. A canceled non-attack action still advances. Suppressing a preparation action does not remove a separately scheduled next-turn bonus: for example, canceling Basking Jaws' Ward turn does not cancel the next snap's +3. No hidden charge status is implied.
• For top-card effects, reshuffle discard when draw is empty, as in the main rules. If both are empty, do nothing. Moved cards preserve their faces. Player-chosen card moves show targets; no legal target means that trait clause does nothing. When an enemy moves multiple kept cards to the top, the player chooses the order.
• Conditional effects change the intent preview as cards are played. Before ending the turn, show each hit after Weaken, Ward removed or ignored, the predicted HP loss, healing/Ward afterward, and any card movement. Keep the conditions readable outside the art.
• No enemy HP, unlock order or finalized run difficulty is assigned here. The existing five-fight route remains a separate fixture; this broader roster proposes different rhythms for some of those enemies. The parent task should reconcile them before implementing a route.

THOUGHT EXPERIMENTS — MANUAL REASONING, NOT PLAYTEST RESULTS

• Porcelain Choir and Weaken: Three Voices: 3 hits of 2 Binding; player has Weaken 3 and 3 Ward. Hits become 0, 2, 2. Ward absorbs 3 total; player loses 1 HP. Current Hush cannot be played against the attack. Multi-hit pressure makes Ward and Weaken meaningfully different without bypassing the damage rules.
• Dentist extracts the shield: Ivory Dentist: 4 Release damage; player has 7 Ward and no Weaken. Extraction removes 5 Ward, leaving 2. The hit consumes those 2 and deals 2 HP damage. Weaken 4 would prevent the HP loss. The pull has intuitive counterplay; extra small shields are inefficient, but all damage still has one type.
• Basking is canceled: Hush cancels the Crocodile's displayed Bask action. It gains no 3 Ward and advances to its attack. Its next scheduled snap is still 8 Radiance, because the trait uses a fixed rhythm, not a stored charge. Preview this explicitly; if players expect Hush to remove the next bonus too, revise the trait to use a charge or simplify it.
• Burn before the ash bed: Ashen Gardener has 3 Burn and 0 Ward at player turn end. It first takes 3 Flame damage and Burn falls to 2. If alive, it attacks, then removes the remaining 2 Burn and gains 2 Ward. Burn is never retroactively prevented; the conversion is a visible trait, not a resistance.
• Wax seals a carried hand: The player keeps two cards, then the Wax Regent deals HP damage. Wax Seal puts those physical cards on top of draw in the player's chosen order. Next turn they are part of the normal five-card draw, not two extra carried cards. The sticky seal delays the benefit of Keep without permanently destroying cards.
• Raven copies strength: Player uses a 9-damage direct attack, then a 4-damage direct attack against Painted Raven. Its Memory hit becomes 4; if the 9 were last it would be capped at 8. Weaken is applied afterward. It never copies both damage types or the attack's heal. Play order creates a readable choice; damage remains single-type.
• Ward does not carry through quiet turns: Player gains 6 Ward against an enemy's non-attack action. Unused player Ward expires next turn. The roster therefore does not treat merely removing player Ward on a quiet turn as a threatening trait. Ward-stripping traits now act immediately before attacks, where they have a real effect.
• No Keep in the pool: Saffron Spider faces a deck with no Keep cards. Its second fang never triggers; it becomes a 3-damage single-hit foe. Keep-responsive enemies belong in a pool where Keep is obtainable; do not count their full trait pressure in a starter fixture.
• Untyped utility is not a weakness attack: A Frost-weak enemy faces the starter's Frost face, which only grants Ward. Frost deals no direct damage and earns no +2 bonus. Flame enemies need a wider reward pool containing actual Frost attacks; thematic names alone are insufficient.
• An arrest without a valid card: On the Amber Bailiff's third attack turn, the last player card played Banishes itself. It is already outside discard, so Amber Custody takes no card. Otherwise the exact last played physical card is banished for this fight and returns afterward. A visible last-play target gives the player a deliberate way to protect a vital pair.
