# Binding and Release: focused review

At `2b59774`, the live catalogue contains 12 Binding-damage card faces and nine Release-damage faces. The supported enemy catalogue has 36 enemies with Binding attack patterns and six with a Binding weakness; six have Release attack patterns and 36 a Release weakness. These counts include Ward-piercing attacks. The terms are colored damage-type keywords: neither applies a separate status, but the matching enemy weakness adds 2 to the first hit. The established design marks both names provisional and removes the old opposing-theme taxonomy.

## Artwork sampled

These are individual full-size WebP views, not a complete review of all affected content:

- **Binding:** Stalactite shows a sharp hanging rock, Iron Boot a heavy boot, and Iron Needlecase a closed metal case. Their damage reads more like falling, crushing or piercing than binding. The Knotted Giant is visibly bound in ribbons and its Binding attack is a more plausible thematic fit. The Fogbound Weaver's threads likewise make Binding intuitive for its attack.
- **Release:** Geyser shows an eruption, and Broken Dam a surge of water; release of pressure is a plausible interpretation. Drawn Blade, Harpoon and Golden Shears show cutting or piercing objects: their damage is apparent, but the word Release does not describe the hit naturally. Sunbleached Harlequin has a Release attack; the luminous acrobat does not itself explain that type.

## Recommendation

Do not make a global rename now. Decide first whether damage types should describe the **physical way a hit hurts** or retain poetic associations. The sampled artwork favors physical descriptions for immediate comprehension, but it does not support one single replacement name for each current group: an impact label could suit a heavy boot or falling rock, while a cutting or piercing label could suit a blade or harpoon; neither naturally describes every geyser, net, thread or wound in the existing groups. These are examples for a future design exercise, not new approved types.

A mechanical one-for-one rename of both attacks and weaknesses could preserve existing weakness bonuses, but would leave mismatched artwork under a new name. Reassigning individual attacks to existing or new types would change which enemies take the +2 bonus, including the Weaver's current Release weakness, and could make some weaknesses hard to exploit. Evaluate card artwork and enemy weaknesses together, preserve one type per hit, inspect in-game keyword presentation and test representative saved runs and builds. Retaining the names avoids that rebalance but keeps the clarity issue. Do not treat the counts above as proof of actual encounter frequency or balance.
