## Watchtower damage-type correction

The tower's broken wooden structure is a clearer visual cue for Decay than for Release, a provisional name the design specifically marks for reconsideration. Collapsed Watchtower now deals 6 Decay damage and Banishes itself; the one-use impact and scouting reverse are otherwise unchanged. This changes weakness matching: in a constructed browser fight the Decay-weak Bellflower Magistrate took 8 damage, while the Release-weak Weaver no longer gets that bonus. A separate unit check verifies both outcomes with 2 Ward and an existing save. The browser also verified the Decay tooltip and save/resume. These are focused scenarios, not evidence of balance across encounters.

Release still appears as a damage-type/colored keyword in the playable runtime: nine other card faces, 36 supported enemies' weaknesses and six supported enemies' attack patterns use it. It has no independent status behavior; its gameplay effect is type matching for weakness. Binding and Release are both provisional names in the design. A broader rename, removal or reassignment requires reviewing the affected artwork, damage descriptions, enemy weaknesses, UI and balance together; no such rules change was made here.

## Tenth pass: self-Keep decisions

At `1d81889`, the actual Anchor, Kite, Confession, Secrecy, Key and Lock WebPs were inspected again. The first two pairs had been offered or reviewed in earlier play; Key and Lock are starting cards. A self-Keep-only play costs a mana to retain its source, which can preserve its opposite face for a later Flip but provides no immediate defense, damage or card advantage. The last manual expedition skipped Confession & Secrecy, and Anchor did not appear, so their natural selection frequency and balance remain unmeasured.

Provisionally, Anchor now says “Keep your hand.” The great anchor holds the other cards in place; the source itself is discarded. Kite still draws two for immediate options. Secrecy now says “Hush. Banish this card.” The figure's silence gesture gives the face an immediate use against an enemy trait, and self-Banish prevents repeatable suppression. Confession retains its discard-for-healing cost. Both changes apply to existing saves. Lock remains self-Keep-only: it is paired with the common Key, whose draw-and-Flip makes the retained face a possible future timing choice; no clear replacement was established in this pass.

The new effects repeat other pairs' visible faces, including Sealed Gate and Silence. Reward selection prefers visibly distinct effects when the available pool permits but can still show duplicates; the changes improve these two pairs without resolving catalogue-wide repetition. In a controlled browser fight, Anchor kept three other cards; Secrecy silenced the Mole's bypass and was Banished; playing Sun and resuming after reload retained Angel and prevented damage on the enemy turn. Unit tests compare Anchor with Kite and check Secrecy's valid and invalid intents. These are constructed examples, not natural-run balance evidence. Other self-Keep-only faces remain for individual review; no broader rule changed.

A subsequent natural light Seraph expedition reached three reward screens after the Jackal, a Carousel combat detour and the Celestial Fisher. Neither revised pair appeared among those nine choices. Chessboard was chosen and played in the Fisher fight, but this sample cannot validate natural play or reward frequency for Anchor and Secrecy; the controlled fight above remains the direct evidence for them.

## Fourteenth pass: removing or bypassing Ward

At `2986b69`, the Dusk Fisher, Ivory Dentist and Sable Locksmith artwork was individually inspected at full image size and their action descriptions compared with live combat. The fisher reels a line from a cliff, the dentist holds forceps and an extracted tooth, and the locksmith opens a lock with a key. Pulling away Ward fits the first two images; bypassing Ward fits the locksmith opening a defense. Their provisional damage-type names remain part of the separate open question. No enemy mechanics were changed.

In controlled Chromium fights with 5 player Ward, the Fisher removed 4 before its 4-damage hit, causing 3 HP damage; the Dentist removed all 5 and caused 4 HP damage. Hush prevented the removal and Ward absorbed the hit; Freeze skipped the action. With Curse reducing the hit to zero, the removal still occurred. Reflect redirected the hit after the player's Ward had already been removed. By contrast, the Locksmith's 5-damage bypass left the 5 Ward intact during the hit, after which normal end-turn halving left 2. Hush removed that bypass, Freeze skipped the action, and Reflect redirected the bypassing hit. These constructed scenarios test selected interactions rather than natural encounter frequency or balance.

The full intent tooltip already described the Fisher's and Dentist's traits, but their compact intent omitted the pre-hit Ward removal. It now adds “remove up to 4 Ward first” or the corresponding amount, and omits that cue when Hush suppresses the trait or Freeze skips the action. Browser checks compare the tooltip and compact intent with actual HP, Ward and enemy HP for normal, Hush, Freeze, Curse and Reflect cases at a 320 × 480 viewport. No damage-type, Bless/Curse or Freeze-thaw rule changed.
