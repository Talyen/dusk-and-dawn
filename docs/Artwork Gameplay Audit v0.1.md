# Artwork and gameplay audit

This is a continuing, explicitly partial audit of the current playable runtime. The entries below were individually viewed as the actual WebP artwork, then compared with the printed runtime effects and their handlers at `e6caa9c`. Notes reflect thematic judgment, not proof of balance or independent verification of every interaction. Earlier catalogue reviews have broader thumbnail coverage; this list records only the images inspected in this pass.

At `bb1902f`, four pairs that had the identical two-face outcome of Scry-and-draw or Curse 2 were examined as eight individual WebP images alongside their live handlers and earlier thematic notes. This is a focused subset, not a claim of catalogue-wide visual review.

| Pair                              | Artwork and assessment                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dream & Nightmare                 | A closed-eyed figure towers over a dreamscape; the darker face shows threatening shadows around a sleeping figure. Scry-and-draw fits foresight through dreaming; Curse is a plausible weakening from fear.                                   |
| Compass & Labyrinth               | A bright compass points a route; the reverse shows a traveler in a winding maze. Scry-and-draw fits navigation, while Curse suggests an enemy losing its way.                                                                                 |

| Watchtower & Collapsed Watchtower | A high illuminated tower watches the road; the reverse shows a broken, leaning tower. Scouting fits the intact tower. The prior Curse represented obstructing the enemy with wreckage, but its collapse also makes an immediate impact clear. |

The intact Watchtower keeps Scry-and-draw. The first provisional version gave Collapsed Watchtower 6 Release damage and self-Banish, turning the collapsing structure into a single impact instead of repeating the other three pairs' Curse. It needed no targeting prompt. In that initial browser battle, the intact face drew Sun; the collapsed face dealt 8 damage to the Release-weak Weaver, disappeared from hand, and remained spent after reload. That damage type was subsequently revised as described below. Existing saves retain the card and use its current effect. The Watchtower pair is available in the tier 3 reward pool; this constructed encounter is not evidence of how often it is offered or of long-run balance. A restricted 40-seed reward check verifies the revised pair is retained alongside otherwise identical scouting pairs.

The three retained pairs remain fully equivalent mechanically; their differences are visual. Broad reward sampling and natural runs are still needed to assess whether further differentiation is worthwhile. Bless/Curse scaling was not changed.

## Watchtower damage-type correction

The tower's broken wooden structure is a clearer visual cue for Decay than for Release, a provisional name the design specifically marks for reconsideration. Collapsed Watchtower now deals 6 Decay damage and Banishes itself; the one-use impact and scouting reverse are otherwise unchanged. This changes weakness matching: in a constructed browser fight the Decay-weak Bellflower Magistrate took 8 damage, while the Release-weak Weaver no longer gets that bonus. A separate unit check verifies both outcomes with 2 Ward and an existing save. The browser also verified the Decay tooltip and save/resume. These are focused scenarios, not evidence of balance across encounters.

Release still appears as a damage-type/colored keyword in the playable runtime: nine other card faces, 36 supported enemies' weaknesses and six supported enemies' attack patterns use it. It has no independent status behavior; its gameplay effect is type matching for weakness. Binding and Release are both provisional names in the design. A broader rename, removal or reassignment requires reviewing the affected artwork, damage descriptions, enemy weaknesses, UI and balance together; no such rules change was made here.

## Fifth pass: violin event and repeated Curse

A hands-on tier 3 light-start Thorn Witch expedition at `071ac3e` took the event offering Tuned Violin & Broken Violin after the opening Moth. In the Duchess fight, Tuned Violin and Censer both offered Curse 2; in the Stag fight, Tuned Violin, Censer and Ivy appeared together with that same visible effect. The original Broken Violin offered only a larger one-use Curse. This is a real example of repetition in one run, not an estimate of its frequency.

The actual artwork shows an intact red violin beneath a radiant figure, and a snapped, silent instrument with broken strings on the reverse. Provisionally, Tuned Violin now says “Bless 1. Draw 1 card.”: the melody inspires and draws another option. Broken Violin says “Hush. Banish this card.”: the broken instrument becomes silent, without an extra target prompt. Both remain subject to the existing Bless and Hush rules; Bless/Curse scaling is unchanged. The pair no longer has the same outcomes as its original faces, and existing saves use the current effects.

After a full reload during the expedition, the Witch defeated the Hollow King at 29/30 HP, having also taken the Dusk Fisher combat detour. In that boss fight the revised Broken Violin silenced Empty Decree and prevented its library Banish. One earlier click on Tuned Violin immediately after hot module replacement still used the old running session handler despite the updated label; the session was then reloaded, so that click is not evidence for the revised effect. A separate constructed browser scenario played the new Tuned Violin, its drawn Sun and Broken Violin against the Stag, resumed after reload and verified the attack and suppressed trait. Unit checks cover no drawable card, the hand cap, existing saves and invalid Hush; these scenarios do not establish balance.

The Frozen Lake and Boiling Lake WebPs were also viewed: ice and steam correspond to their Frost/suppression and Fire/Burn effects. Cathedral and Ruined Cathedral were viewed as well; the intact church's Ward is plausible protection, while recovering a discarded card from the ruined building is a looser cue. Their effects were not changed in this pass. The Celestial Fisher enemy image was viewed at the route choice, but that encounter was not selected; no mechanic assessment is claimed for it.
